Getting More Years From Your Water Heater in Springfield

Many early failures start with skipped maintenance, and the habits that prevent them are easy. Drain and flush the tank every year to clear sediment, have the anode rod inspected every few years, and test the relief valve annually so it doesn't seize. Keep the temperature near 120 degrees to reduce scald risk and slow scale buildup. For a tankless heater in a hard-water area, plan on descaling once a year to protect the heat exchanger.

How old is your water heater? The rating plate on the tank usually includes the month and year it was built, and its age tells you a lot. Most tank heaters reach 8 to 12 years, heat pump models 10 to 15, and regularly descaled tankless units about 20. If your heater is past that range and is also showing rusty water, popping sounds, or moisture at the base, it's a strong candidate for a planned replacement, which is almost always cheaper and easier on everyone than a rushed emergency install after a leak.

The sooner you call about a hot water problem, the smaller the repair tends to be. A tank that's just begun to rumble can often be fixed with a flush, yet that same buildup, ignored through a few more years, can create hot spots on the tank floor and burn out the lower element on an electric model, forcing a replacement much sooner. Save our number in your contacts now so help is one call away the moment something sounds or looks wrong. A quick call saves time, money, and ruined floors.
